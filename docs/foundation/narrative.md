Project: SUPPLY LINE | Narrative & World Design

> **Status:** Full vision, applies to all versions. Narrative direction is version-independent.

## I. Tone

Warm, whimsical, melancholic. A Ghibli fantasy — the world is beautiful and broken, the tower is alive and tired, the journey is about finding belonging. War exists but isn't the point. The point is: you built something worth protecting, and you're walking it somewhere safe.

The game should make you feel:
- **Cozy** when your tower is humming and your companions are chatting between encounters
- **Protective** when enemies approach — not "time to kill" but "stay away from my home"
- **Bittersweet** when you lose a floor or a companion is displaced — not rage, but sadness
- **Hopeful** when you repair, rebuild, recruit someone new, see the next stretch of road

**What we're NOT:** dark, grimdark, gritty, cynical, ironic, nihilistic. The world has problems. People have hardship. But the response to hardship is building, walking, persisting — not brooding.

---

## II. The World

The world was once connected by great roads. Towns traded freely. Then the roads became dangerous — wild creatures, territorial spirits, roaming hazards. Travel became impossible. Towns turned inward. Communities fragmented. The world didn't end — it just stopped moving.

Walking towers were the answer. Somewhere in the past, someone figured out how to make a building walk. Maybe it was magic, maybe it was engineering, maybe it was a spirit that chose to carry a house on its back. The knowledge spread, mutated, adapted. Now there are walkers everywhere — some are merchants, some are couriers, some are wanderers, some are homes for people who have nowhere else.

**The world is not at war.** There is no evil empire, no dark lord, no invading army. The enemies you encounter are:
- **Territorial creatures** that live in the landscape and attack anything that enters their territory. They're not evil — they're animals and spirits defending their home. The tower walks THROUGH their territory. You're the intruder.
- **Bandits and desperate people** who target walkers for their cargo. Poverty, not malice.
- **Corrupted spirits** in certain regions — the land itself is unhealthy, and the spirits that inhabit it have become aggressive. This is the closest thing to a "villain" — but it's an environmental problem, not a character.
- **Other walkers** occasionally — rival towers, territorial, competitive. Not common.

The enemies are obstacles on a journey, not an opposing force. The game never asks you to "defeat the enemy." It asks you to "survive the journey."

## III. The Tower

The tower is alive. Not in a "it talks and has opinions" way (it doesn't have dialogue), but in a "it breathes, it hums, it resists intruders" way. The tower's enchantment — the thing that makes it walk, the thing that expels raiders — is a spirit that inhabits the structure. Not a character. A presence.

**The tower's spirit:**
- It was here before you. The tower existed, walking aimlessly, empty. Empty towers aren't rare — spirits outlive their crews, and most people pass them by. You didn't. You moved in. You started building inside it. The spirit tolerates you because you take care of it — you build floors (its body grows), you repair panels (you heal its wounds), you feed it materials (it gets stronger).
- The spirit's enchantment is what makes the legs move, what makes the walls subtly repel enemies, what expels raiders after 15-20 seconds. It's not magic you control — it's the tower's own immune system. You benefit from it but you don't command it.
- When the tower is damaged, it feels it. The spirit's hum dims. The walking becomes slower, more labored. When you repair, the hum returns. The relationship between you and the tower is symbiotic — you're its caretaker, it's your home.
- The "Tower Dream" mystery event (where the tower offers you a choice: heal all panels OR +1 cache capacity) is the spirit's instinct surfacing — not a conversation, but a reflex. Rare, significant, slightly eerie. Companions may read intent into it, but the spirit doesn't explain itself.

**Why the tower walks:** it has always walked. It was made to walk, or it chose to walk. The spirit inside it needs to move — staying still too long makes it dormant, and a dormant tower is just a building. Walking is breathing. The journey is the tower's natural state.

**A note on the spirit's "personality":** companions will attribute feelings and preferences to the tower — Bell hears it "pulling south," Yuki says the walls are "stressed." This is interpretation, not fact. The tower does not speak, want, or choose in any way the player can verify. Whether the spirit truly has preferences or the companions are projecting is an open question — and should stay open.

## IV. The Hero

You are a **walker-keeper** — someone who lives in and cares for a walking tower. This isn't a military role. It's closer to a lighthouse keeper, a shipwright, a farmer. You maintain the tower, build its interior, tend its supply chains, and defend it when the road gets dangerous.

**You're not a chosen one.** You're not special. You're someone who found an empty walking tower and decided to make it a home. Your skills with a bow (or crossbow, or hammer, or lute) are practical, not heroic. You fight because the road requires it, not because you seek battle.

**Class identities in narrative terms:**
- **Archer:** a hunter. Practical, patient, observant. Joined the tower because the wilderness was getting too dangerous to travel alone. Good at making one arrow count.
- **Engineer:** a builder. Came from a town that was dying — no trade, no supplies. Took their skills and walked. The tower is their greatest project. Fights reluctantly but builds brilliantly.
- **Commander:** a former caravan leader. Used to organizing people and logistics. Lost their caravan to the road. Found the tower and started over with a new crew. Fights by empowering others.
- **Scavenger:** a drifter. Has been walking alone for years, picking through ruins and abandoned places. Joined the tower because it was warm and it walked. Fights messily but profitably.

The hero doesn't have a detailed backstory or extensive dialogue. They're a vessel for the player. But the class choice provides a flavor — why you're here, what you value.

## V. Companions

Companions are people the tower picks up along the way. They're refugees, wanderers, adventurers, outcasts — people who need a home and are willing to work for it. They join the tower because they have nowhere else to go, and they stay because it becomes their home too.

**Each companion has:**
- A name
- A personality (expressed through combat barks, prep phase comments, recruitment dialogue)
- A reason they're on the road (why they need a home)
- A preferred position/playstyle (expressed through their recruitment dialogue and suggestions)
- A relationship with the tower that grows over time (more comments, warmer tone as encounters increase)

### The Roster (~15 companions, see 6-8 per run)

**Defenders:**

**Ren, the Spotter**
- Passive: Mark (+20% damage from all sources on hit enemies)
- Weapon preference: crossbow
- Personality: quiet, precise, observant. Speaks rarely but always says something useful.
- Story: was a surveyor who had mapped every inch of their province twice over. When the roads closed, the work dried up — but the restlessness didn't. Joined the tower because the horizon still had unmapped edges.
- Quote: "I don't need to talk. I need to see."
- Prep hint style: terse, tactical. "Flyers north. Crossbow weather."

**Kael, the Shield-Bearer**
- Passive: Wall (blocks climbers at position)
- Weapon preference: none (doesn't shoot)
- Personality: stoic, protective, steady. The quiet anchor of any crew.
- Story: was a town gate guard who spent years watching the road from behind iron bars. When the town sealed its gates for good, everyone else felt safe. Kael felt buried. Walked out the day before they welded the hinges shut.
- Quote: "Nothing gets past me. That's the deal."
- Prep hint style: practical. "Floor 3 panel is cracking. Should fix that."

**Mira, the Grenadier**
- Passive: Splash (AoE damage, 2x ammo consumption)
- Weapon preference: bombs, thrown
- Personality: loud, cheerful, slightly reckless. Finds explosions beautiful.
- Story: was a quarry demolition worker. Got bored blowing up rocks. Heard walkers needed people who could blow up other things. Applied.
- Quote: "You know what fixes everything? A bigger bomb."
- Prep hint style: enthusiastic. "Oh! I see a forge site on the map! Can we go? Please?"

**Yuki, the Healer**
- Passive: Patch (passively repairs tower exterior near position between encounters)
- Weapon preference: staff (gentle, supportive)
- Personality: warm, worried, caring. Treats the tower like a patient.
- Story: has always been a caretaker — first animals, then people, then a village clinic that served fewer patients every season. Didn't leave because the village failed. Left because she realized she was staying out of habit, not purpose. The tower gave her something that actually needs tending.
- Quote: "The wall on floor 4 is stressed. I can hear it."
- Prep hint style: nurturing. "We should rest. Everyone — including the tower — needs it."

**Drift, the Archer**
- Passive: Pinning Shots (hit enemies slowed 1 second)
- Weapon preference: bow
- Personality: easy-going, wandering, philosophical. Has been on many towers.
- Story: has been walking for years. Not running from anything, not going anywhere. Just walking. Joins towers for company, leaves when the road calls. Might leave yours someday.
- Quote: "I'll stay as long as the walking's good."
- Prep hint style: reflective. "The mountain path is hard. But the view is worth it."

- Quote: "That weapon is worth twice what you paid. Good deal."

**Thorn, the Whip-Fighter**

- Weapon preference: whip
- Personality: theatrical, dramatic, vain. Treats combat as performance.

**Specialists:**

**Bell, the Musician**

- Weapon preference: any instrument
- Personality: serene, otherworldly, slightly disconnected. Hears the tower's spirit more clearly than others.

- Personality: weathered, laconic, pragmatic. Few words, all of them matter.

**Wild Cards:**

**Kit, the Inventor**
- Passive: Jury-Rig (once per encounter, one broken piece of infrastructure auto-repairs temporarily for the rest of the encounter)
- Weapon preference: any (keeps changing, can't decide)
- Personality: hyperactive, curious, easily distracted. Always tinkering.
- Story: was expelled from an engineering guild for "unsafe innovations." Their inventions work. They just also occasionally explode.

- Story: was a mountain hermit. The mountain started crumbling. Stone walked down and kept walking. Found the tower and it felt like a mountain that moves.
- Quote: "The tower is strong. Like a mountain. But better. Mountains don't walk."
- Prep hint style: simple, grounded. "Big things coming. I can feel the ground shaking."

## VI. The Journey

**Why are you traveling?**

The hero's starting town is failing. Trade has stopped. Supplies are dwindling. People are leaving. The hero found the tower — an empty walking fortress — on the outskirts. They decided to walk it somewhere better. Somewhere the roads are still open. Somewhere trade still flows. Somewhere that feels like home.

The destination isn't a specific plot point — it's a place of hope. Each journey destination (3-4 options) represents a different rumored "safe place":

**The Harbor** — a coastal city that still trades by sea. If you can reach it, you can trade. The journey goes through lowlands, swamps, and coast. The final boss occupies the harbor approach — a territorial sea-spirit that treats the inlet as its own.

**The Crossroads** — a legendary intersection of old roads, maintained by an order of road-keepers. If you can reach it, the roads open again. The journey goes through heartland, mountains, and overgrown passes. The final boss is a corrupted road spirit.

**The Grove** — a forested sanctuary where the land spirits are still healthy. If you can reach it, the tower's spirit will be renewed. The journey goes through dying forests and reviving ones. The final boss is the decayed spirit of the outermost forest, blocking entry.

**The Forge (unlockable)** — the legendary workshop where the first walking towers were built. If you can reach it, you can make the tower stronger than any other. The hardest journey — through industrial ruins, mountain passes, and ancient defenses. The final boss is the forge's guardian, testing whether you're worthy.

Each destination gives the run a narrative frame without requiring cutscenes or exposition dumps. The hero is walking toward hope. The companions comment on the landscape, the destination, the journey. The story emerges from the walk.

## VII. Narrative Delivery — Show, Don't Tell

The game has almost no exposition. No cutscenes. No text crawl. The narrative is delivered through:

**Companion dialogue** — short, contextual lines during prep and post-combat. These are the primary narrative vehicle. Companions comment on the landscape, the tower's state, each other, the journey. Over time, their comments become warmer (they're settling in, this is becoming home).

**The tower's visual state** — a healthy, humming tower IS the story. A damaged, quiet tower IS the story. The emotional arc of a run is told through the soundscape and the visual state of the cross-section.

**The landscape** — as the tower walks, the terrain tells a story. Dead forests give way to living ones (if you're heading to the Grove). Industrial ruins show the remnants of a connected world (if heading to the Forge). The map is the narrative — each path you choose is a chapter.

**Loot flavor text** — weapons and trinkets have brief descriptions that hint at the world. "A crossbow from the harbor watchtower. The sea salt has jammed the mechanism — but the aim is true." One line, evocative, not lore-dumpy.

**Mystery events** — the Tower Dream, the Collector, the Cursed Shrine. These are micro-stories that happen along the road. Brief, memorable, slightly mysterious.

**The merchant** — each merchant archetype has personality. The Arms Dealer has seen many towers fall ("You want the good stuff? You'll need it."). The Supplier is worried about you ("Please take extra planks. The mountains are rough."). The Collector is enigmatic ("I collect interesting things. You are... interesting.").

**What we DON'T do:**
- No opening cutscene. The game starts with you in a tower that starts walking. That's the tutorial.
- No lore codex or journal. If you want to know the world, listen to your companions.
- No dialogue trees or player choices in conversation. Companions talk, you listen. Your choices are mechanical (where to go, what to build, who to recruit), not conversational.
- No villain monologue. No final boss speech. The boss is a creature or spirit in the way. You fight it because it's between you and your destination.

## VIII. Emotional Arc of a Run

**Encounters 1-5: Discovery.** The tower is small, the road is new. Companions are strangers making small talk. The world is pretty but empty. "Where are we going?" "Somewhere better."

**Encounters 6-12: Building.** The tower is growing. Companions start relaxing, making jokes, giving advice. The first hard fight bonds the crew. The tower starts feeling like home. "This floor needs a forge." "I'll handle the left side. You watch the right."

**Encounters 13-20: Midpoint.** The journey is long. The landscape changes. Some encounters are hard. The tower takes damage. Companions worry but also encourage. "We've come too far to turn back." "The tower's strong. WE'RE strong."

**Encounters 21-30: Trial.** The hardest stretch. The tower is tested. Breaches happen. Repairs are costly. But the crew is experienced — veterans who know each other's rhythms. Companions' dialogue becomes warmer, more familiar. "Same as yesterday. You build, I'll shoot." "Cover me — the rack's refilling."

**Encounters 31+: Arrival.** The destination is visible on the map. The final stretch. Companions talk about what they'll do when they arrive. The tower is a machine, humming, battle-scarred, alive. The final boss is the last obstacle. Everything you built — every floor, every lift, every companion bond — is tested one last time.

**Victory:** the tower reaches the destination. Brief moment — the tower stops walking. The landscape is different. The companions step outside. A line of dialogue from each companion about what this place means to them. The tower's spirit hums a new note — it's home. Fade to credits.

Not a dramatic climax. A quiet arrival. The journey was the game. The arrival is the exhale.

**Failure:** the tower collapses. The spirit goes dormant — not dead, but still. The companions scatter back to the road, alone again. No death screen, no game over fanfare. Just the sound of the spirit's hum fading, and the companions walking away in different directions. The tragedy isn't dying. It's losing the home you built together. The feeling should be the same sadness as line 10 — not rage, but grief for something that was working and stopped.
